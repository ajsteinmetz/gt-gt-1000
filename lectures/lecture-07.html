What is LaTeX, and why Overleaf?

  • LaTeX is plain text with markup, compiled into a PDF.
  • You write what you mean; LaTeX does the layout.
  • Physicists use it for equations, numbering, cross-references, and bibliographies.
  • Overleaf is LaTeX in your browser. Free with your GT email; nothing to install.

Anatomy of a LaTeX document

\documentclass[12pt, letterpaper]{article}
\usepackage{amsmath}   % better math
\usepackage{graphicx}  % images
\title{PHYS 2211, HW 5, Problem 3}
\author{Your Name}
\date{\today}

\begin{document}
\maketitle
\section{Problem Statement}
A ball is launched at speed $v_0$ and
angle $\theta$ above level ground.
Find its \textbf{range} $R$.
\end{document}
  • Preamble: class, packages, title.
  • Body: what appears on the page.
  • Commands start with \.
  • {} holds arguments; [] holds options.
  • Environments: \begin … \end.
  • % starts a comment.

Source: (Oetiker et al. 2021)

The preamble is everything before \begin{document}. Blank lines separate paragraphs; extra spaces and single line breaks are ignored. LaTeX ignores everything after a % on that line.

Worked problem (PHYS 2211 style): a ball is launched from level ground with speed v0 at angle theta. Find its range R.

Step 4, list of given quantities:

\begin{itemize}
  \item $v_0$: launch speed (m/s)
  \item $\theta$: launch angle above the horizontal
  \item $g = 9.8~\text{m/s}^2$: gravitational acceleration
\end{itemize}

Step 5, the equation and a reference back to it:

The ball lands when $y = 0$, at $t = 2 v_0 \sin\theta / g$.
Multiplying by the horizontal speed $v_0 \cos\theta$ gives
\begin{equation}
  R = \frac{v_0^2 \sin 2\theta}{g}.
  \label{eq:range}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:range} is largest at $\theta = 45^\circ$.

Step 6, the figure:

\begin{figure}[h]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.5\textwidth]{trajectory.png}
  \caption{Trajectory of the ball.}
  \label{fig:traj}
\end{figure}

Step 7, three breaks: (1) type “100% of the time” in a sentence; there is no error, the rest of the line silently disappears. Fix with a backslash before the percent sign. (2) Type v_0 outside math and show “Missing $ inserted.” (3) Delete a closing brace and show how the log points to the line.

Interpretation section (say it, maybe type it): units are (m/s)^2 / (m/s^2) = m; R = 0 at theta = 0 and at 90 degrees; v0 = 10 m/s at 45 degrees gives about 10.2 m, which is reasonable for a thrown ball.

When it won’t compile

Message Usual cause Fix
Undefined control sequence Typo in a command, or a missing package Check spelling; add the \usepackage
Missing $ inserted ^, _, or Greek outside math Wrap it in $...$
Runaway argument A { with no matching } Find and close the brace
Text vanishes after % % starts a comment Write \% (also \&, \$, \#, \_)
File not found Image not uploaded, or the name differs Match the file name exactly

Recompile often. Fix the first error first; the log gives you the line number.

Overleaf’s log panel links each error to its line in the editor. Errors often cascade: one missing brace can produce ten messages, and fixing the first one clears the rest.

Writing up a solution

  • Write for a reader who has not seen the assignment.
  • Define every symbol the first time it appears, with units.
  • Put words between equations. Equations are part of sentences and take punctuation.
  • Check your answer, units, size, and limiting cases.
  • Give every figure a caption and refer to it in text.

Less effective

\(y = v_0 \sin\theta \, t - \tfrac{1}{2} g t^2 = 0\)
\(t = 2 v_0 \sin\theta / g\)
\(R = v_0^2 \sin 2\theta / g\)

More effective

The ball lands when its height returns to zero, \(v_0 \sin\theta \, t - \tfrac{1}{2} g t^2 = 0\), so its flight time is \(t = 2 v_0 \sin\theta / g\). Multiplying by its horizontal speed \(v_0 \cos\theta\) gives the range \[R = \frac{v_0^2 \sin 2\theta}{g}. \tag{1}\] At \(\theta = 0\) the range is zero, as expected, and it is largest at \(\theta = 45^\circ\).
